The sixteen pieces the rest is built from.

Every other page in this showcase is a combination of a few small, separable ideas: a world with a camera, geometry made of triangles, transforms and interpolation, transparency, and text projected onto the scene. Each demo below isolates one of them — with the API it uses written underneath the title — so you can read it, then reuse it.

01

The 3D coordinate system

axes3d(size, width) · boundsBox(min, max) · LabelLayer · OrbitCamera

FRAME
drag to orbit · scroll to zoom

Three arrows from one origin, integer ticks, a cage at the end of each axis and a grid floor: everything in the library is drawn in this frame. Labels are DOM elements projected through the same camera each frame, so they stay crisp at any zoom.

02

Interpolation, three ways

Timeline · tween · smooth · lerp

MOTION

One Timeline drives all three markers from the same clock: the first reads lerp (constant speed), the second smooth (slow at both ends), the third tween with a delay. Seeking is absolute, so scrubbing, replaying and pausing never drift.

03

Solids with transparent sides

box · sphere · cylinder · rgba(hex, alpha) · Group

VOLUME

A box, a ball and a rod, all translucent, one inside another. Each is a closed surface — six quads, a parametric ball, a cylinder with caps — and the renderer draws opaque geometry first and sorts translucent faces back to front, so the nesting reads correctly no matter which was added first. A wire outline on each keeps its shape legible through the faces.

04

Geometry, made and merged

polyline · arrow · circle · sphere · wireSphere · parametricSurface · merge · orientation

SHAPES

Geometry is unindexed triangles with optional per-vertex colours, and it is immutable and shareable. Primitives compose with merge, and a parametric surface is just a function of two parameters — the same code path draws a torus, a Möbius strip and a height field. Turning the shelf uses the node rotation; tilting it sets orientation, an Euler pose applied before it, and the labels ride the same matrix.

05

Groups and nested transforms

Group.add · position · scale · rotation · opacity

HIERARCHY

A group carries a transform that its children inherit, and groups nest: here each arm spins about its own pivot, and each ball spins about the arm's end. Matrices compose on the way down, opacity multiplies, and the renderer walks the tree into one flat list per frame.

06

Labels on the scene

LabelLayer.addHTML · mathml · mi · mn · mo · frac · msup

TEXT

Text is not drawn into the scene: it is HTML positioned by the projected anchor each frame, so it stays sharp, selectable and styleable. The equation under the marker is real MathML from the library's mathml helpers, and its argument updates as the point moves.

07

Colour from data

viridis · plasma · ramp · new Geometry(vertices, colors)

COLOUR

Geometry can carry a linear RGB triple per vertex, which is the whole mechanism behind a colour-mapped surface: the palette is a function from a number in [0,1] to a colour, and the surface is a grid of triangles the demo builds by hand. The ribbon beside it samples the same function, so the encoding is readable.

16

Arrows, and what they add up to

arrow(start, end) · polyline · merge · vec()

DIAGRAM

sum

A diagram is geometry plus text: an arrow is a tube with a cone on the end, the parallelogram is two more tubes, and each vector is named with the math typesetter's vec accent positioned at its tip. Everything follows the two sliders, and the readout restates the arithmetic in the picture.

15

Building a scene in steps

Timeline · Cue · seek · opacity · reveal

STORY

0 · waiting

Every explainer on this site is this demo. One Timeline holds four cues — the frame fades in, the bonds scale out, the atoms arrive, the labels appear — and each cue is a function of its own progress. Seeking is absolute, so the scrubber, the play button and the readout are all the same clock, and a paused frame is reproducible.

14

Streamlines in a vector field

streamlines(field, seeds) · sphereSeeds · polyline · merge

FLOW

A vector field is a function from a point to a direction. streamlines seeds a set of starting points on a sphere and integrates each one with RK4, normalising at every step so the spacing is arc length rather than speed. The tubes are one merged geometry, and the markers riding them are just points interpolated along the traced polylines.

13

Shapes from a scalar field

isosurface(f, bounds, level, resolution) · boundsBox

FIELD

No triangles are authored here: the shape is the set of points where f(x, y, z) = level, extracted on the CPU by marching tetrahedra. Move the level and the surface swells, splits or pinches off — which is why implicit shapes are so easy to animate, and why the field explorer on the landing page can morph one into another.

12

A simulation, stepped by hand

createParticleState · stepParticles · Visual

SIMULATION

The library does not need the GPU for this: stepParticles hands each particle's position and velocity to a function you write, returns an acceleration, and advances semi-implicitly without allocating. Here that function is three lines, and the numbers the readout shows are the same ones driving the picture — six hundred of them at the top of the slider.

11

A camera move

OrbitCamera · yaw · pitch · distance · smooth

CAMERA

A camera is three numbers: yaw, pitch and distance — or the visible height in orthographic mode. Driving them with smooth gives a shot, and scrubbing the same number gives a storyboard of it. Nothing in the scene moves here; the readout below shows the numbers a drag would be editing by hand.

10

One mesh, many copies

Geometry · Group.add · the renderer's batching

BATCHING

The same mesh, moved and scaled rather than rebuilt: 64 cubes here, 216 at the top of the slider, all from one Geometry and two colours. The renderer buckets the tree by geometry and colour and pushes one instanced draw per bucket, which is why a lattice of a thousand atoms costs no more to issue than a handful.

09

Depth, and why order does not matter

box · shadedSphere · rgba(hex, alpha) · two passes

DEPTH

Opacity is only half the problem: translucent faces have to be blended back to front, and the order they were added in must not matter. The renderer draws opaque geometry first with a depth test, then sorts the translucent faces by depth. The spheres below stay solid wherever they sit, and the two slabs blend correctly however they cross — which is what makes nested and interpenetrating solids work everywhere else on this page.

08

A chart in 3D

plotFrame · functionCurve · niceStep · tickValues · formatTick

PLOT

A chart is a 3D scene too, so it orbits, zooms and labels with exactly the same machinery as everything else. plotFrame returns grid, tick and axis geometry plus the anchors to label them, picking round tick steps with niceStep and formatting them with formatTick.
